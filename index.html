Abstract

Robotics is currently undergoing a paradigm shift as recent foundation models enable zero-shot control of robots for manipulation tasks. Current benchmarks for evaluating robot policies ignore high precision and long horizon assembly tasks and focus on computing a binary success rate based on loosely defined goals, such as, placing an object on a centimeter scale goal receptacle region. We introduce RoboBuilder, a suite of short- and long-horizon tasks featuring a diverse collection of assets drawn from the mechanical assembly literature. These tasks span varying levels of manipulation difficulty, requiring robot policies to achieve relative, metric, and constrained placements with millimeter precision. Tasks in RoboBuilder reflect real-world manufacturing challenges and are designed to represent real world applications, where goal specifications are both described by text and/or CAD diagrams. We show that powerful models like GPT 6 Astra do not achieve a high level of accuracy, reporting 1.8% success at 1 mm thresholds on RoboBuilder-Short demonstrating the need for further research on precision manipulation. On RoboBuilder-Long, which tests model capacity to assemble simple objects, GPT 6 Astra with CAD inputs cannot build any of the assemblies within 2 mm and only one assembly at 10 mm. A qualitative real-world study on two 3D-printed assemblies shows that these precision failures carry over to physical robots. We will release our benchmark tasks and metrics to boost community-driven research in improving the precision of future robot policies.
